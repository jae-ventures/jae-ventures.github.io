I was at my favorite local coffee shop the other day thinking about how perfect this would be for EV charging! ☕⚡🔋

Two hours of parking, right next to a major shopping plaza, tons of foot traffic. But like most places I frequent—the grocery store, the park, my favorite coffee shop—there's no charging infrastructure there yet.

As an EV driver, I see missed opportunities everywhere. As someone who writes about city planning, I know the solution can feel daunting for cities.

City planners and leaders: You don't need $100K+ DC Fast chargers to start electrifying your community.

Here's what actually works:

Start with Level 2 charging at places people already spend 1-2 hours:
🔋 Parks, gyms, and recreation centers
🔋 Libraries and community centers
🔋 Downtown retail and dining districts
🔋 Near major employers

Budget: $10-50K for your first 4-8 charging ports

Why this approach works:
☕ Integrates with daily behaviors people already have
☕ Serves apartment dwellers who can't charge at home
☕ Creates visible proof that EVs work in your community
☕ Builds foundation for future expansion

Three key components to EV success:
⚡ Get community feedback first
⚡ Partner with experienced charging providers
⚡ Drive electric yourself to understand what your residents need.

Your city's electrification doesn't start with a massive infrastructure project. It starts with a few well-placed Level 2 chargers where people already park regularly.

City leaders: What's helped your community from install that first charging station? What are your plans for the next?
